# 04. Where to Hunt: Land Access & Permits (*Metsästysoikeus*)

One of the most common misconceptions among newcomers to Finland concerns **Everyman's Right (*Jokamiehenoikeus*)**. While anyone may freely walk, ski, camp, and pick wild berries and mushrooms across any forest in the country, **hunting is strictly excluded**. 

Under Section 6 of the Finnish Hunting Act, **the hunting right belongs exclusively to the landowner**. To hunt anywhere in Finland, you must possess explicit, documented legal authorization for that specific parcel of land.

This chapter breaks down the three pathways to hunting land access: state-owned forests, private hunting clubs, and commercial guided outfitters.

---

## 🌲 1. State-Owned Lands (*Metsähallitus & Eräluvat.fi*)

The Finnish state owns more than **9 million hectares** of pristine boreal forest, tundra, and waterways, managed by the state forestry enterprise, **Metsähallitus**. These public lands provide incredible hunting opportunities, particularly in Eastern Finland (Kainuu, North Karelia) and Northern Finland (Lapland, Kuusamo).

### How It Works via [Eräluvat.fi](https://www.eraluvat.fi)
Any hunter holding a valid Finnish Hunting Card (*Metsästyskortti*) can purchase personal small game permits directly from the Eräluvat web portal.

### Types of State Small Game Permits (*Pienriistaluvat*):
1. **Forest Grouse Permit (*Kanalintulupa*)**:
   - Covers black grouse, capercaillie, hazel grouse, and willow grouse, plus hares, waterfowl, and pest predators.
   - Sold as daily, weekly, or seasonal permits (~**17 € – 25 € per day**).
   - **Quota System**: Because grouse populations fluctuate cyclically, Metsähallitus sets regional hunter-day quotas based on annual wildlife triangle census surveys (*riistakolmiolaskenta*). Permit sales typically open in June.
2. **Waterfowl & Hare Permit (*Vesilintu- ja jänislupa*)**:
   - Covers ducks, geese, and hares in areas where forest bird permits are restricted.
   - Widely available, lower cost (~**10 € – 15 € per day**).
3. **Small Predator Permit (*Pienpetolupa*)**:
   - Extremely affordable (~**10 € for the entire season**).
   - Covers invasive alien species (raccoon dog, American mink) and red fox. Encouraged as public wildlife conservation.
4. **Beaver Permit (*Majavalupa*)**:
   - Area-specific permits for harvesting Canadian beaver or European beaver in spring.

### Key Rules on State Land:
- **Exact Boundaries**: You must stay within the boundary numbers (e.g. Area 5614 "Kuhmo", Area 3608 "Inari"). Download the area map onto the **Eräluvat** or **Oma riista** mobile app before heading into areas without cellular reception.
- **Catch Quotas (*Saaliskiintiö*)**: Permits often limit harvest (e.g., maximum 2 black grouse per day or 1 capercaillie per trip).
- **Mandatory Catch Reporting**: You must log your harvest on Oma riista or Eräluvat within 7 days of the hunt ending.

## 🏡 2. Hunting Clubs (*Metsästysseurat*) on Private Land

In Central, Western, and Southern Finland (where most people reside), the vast majority of land is privately owned by farmers and small woodland owners. 

Because fragmented land parcels (e.g. 10 to 50 hectares) are too small to support sustainable big game hunting, landowners lease their hunting rights to local **hunting clubs (*metsästysseura*)**. A single club typically aggregates private leases to form a contiguous hunting territory of **2,000 to 25,000 hectares**.

### Why Hunting Clubs Matter
- **Big Game Monopoly**: Under Finnish law, a continuous hunting area of at least **1,000 hectares** is required to obtain a moose hunting permit from the Wildlife Agency. Moose and white-tailed deer hunting is therefore almost exclusively practiced through clubs.
- **The Social Core of Finnish Hunting**: Clubs own clubhouses (*seurantalo*), meat processing facilities (*lahtivaja*), cold storage lockers, shooting towers, and shooting ranges. They are where traditions, tracking dogs, and campfire lunches are shared.

### Permanent Resident Rights in Hunting Clubs & Municipalities
As a **Permanent Resident registered with a Finnish home municipality (*kotikunta*)**:
- You are not treated as a "foreign guest." You apply for club membership on the same terms as any local resident.
- **Northern Municipality Free Hunting Right (*Metsästyslaki 8 §*)**: If you reside permanently in Northern Finland (Lapland, Kainuu, Kuusamo/Pudasjärvi/Taivalkoski), Section 8 of the Hunting Act grants all permanent residents the statutory right to hunt small game on state-owned land within their home municipality completely free of charge!
- In Southern and Central Finland, being a resident allows you to join local clubs as a full or small-game member and buy resident-quota permits.

### How Can an Outsider or Beginner Join a Club?
Historically, some rural clubs were closed to non-landowners. Today, demographic shifts have transformed this: **many clubs actively welcome motivated new members**, especially beginners willing to help with club operations.

#### Types of Membership:
- **Full Member (*Varsinainen jäsen*)**: Full rights to all game (including the moose/deer group), voting rights in annual meetings.
- **Small Game Member (*Pienriistajäsen*)**: Access to hunt birds, hares, and predators on club lands without participating in the intensive moose syndicate.
- **Guest / Day Card (*Vieraskortti*)**: A registered member hosts you as their guest for the day (cost typically 10 € – 30 € per day).

#### How to Apply:
1. **Find Clubs in Your Region**: Contact your local Game Management Association (RHY) executive coordinator. Ask which local clubs are open to new members.
2. **Volunteer First**: Offer to help during summer activities: building high seats, clearing shooting lanes, stocking winter feeding stations for deer, or assisting in the butchery during moose season.
3. **Join as a Beater/Driver (*Ajomies*)**: The ultimate fast-track! Clubs are always looking for people willing to walk through the forest drive lines with orange vests. No gun or hunting card required to be a beater, and it instantly demonstrates your dedication.
4. **Fees**:
   - One-off initiation fee (*liittymismaksu*): 100 € – 500 €.
   - Annual membership fee (*vuosimaksu*): 50 € – 150 €.
   - Meat share fee (*lihaosuusmaksu* for big game): covers ammunition for club dogs, refrigeration, and butchery supplies; you receive tens of kilograms of prime moose/deer meat in return.

## 🎯 3. Commercial Outfitters & Guided Day Hunts

If you do not own land, do not belong to a club, and want a turnkey experience with professional mentorship, Finland has a growing network of commercial hunting outfitters:

### Typical Guided Offerings:
1. **Waterfowl Morning & Evening Flights**:
   - Coastal archipelago (Turku archipelago, Porkkala) or lake regions.
   - Guides provide decoy spreads, retrieving Labrador retrievers, camouflage blinds, and safety briefings.
   - Cost: **150 € – 350 € / day**.
2. **High-Seat White-Tailed Deer Hunting (*Kyttäysjahti*)**:
   - Heated tower/stand hunting over regulated grain feed sites in prime deer zones (Varsinais-Suomi, Häme).
   - Guides handle animal field dressing, skinning, and permit quota allocation.
   - Cost: **200 € – 600 €** (trophy bucks may incur additional harvest fees).
3. **Lappish Wilderness Grouse Hunting with Dog**:
   - Multi-day wilderness cabin hunts in Lapland or Kuusamo with pointing dogs (English setters, German pointers) or the native Finnish Spitz (*suomenpystykorva*).
   - Cost: **800 € – 2,000 €** for all-inclusive 3-to-5-day expeditions.

## 🗺️ Land Access Comparison Summary

| Criteria | Metsähallitus (State Land) | Hunting Club (Metsästysseura) | Commercial Guided Hunt |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Region** | Eastern & Northern Finland | Southern & Central Finland | Nationwide |
| **Permitted Game** | Small game, waterfowl, hares, predators | All game (including moose & deer) | Target species per booking |
| **Barrier to Entry** | Lowest (buy online at Eräluvat.fi) | Medium (application & community networking) | Lowest effort, higher financial cost |
| **Cost** | 10 € – 25 € / day | 50 € – 200 € / year + initiation | 150 € – 500 € / day |
| **Mentorship** | Self-guided (must navigate terrain alone) | High (experienced members mentor you) | Maximum (guide handles all logistics) |
