# 01. Legal Framework & The Hunter's Examination (*Metsästäjätutkinto*)

In Finland, hunting is treated as a highly respected, heavily regulated civic responsibility. To be a hunter is to act as an active steward of wildlife populations, forest health, and public safety.

This chapter details the institutions governing Finnish hunting, how to take and pass the Hunter’s Examination (*Metsästäjätutkinto*), and how to obtain your official Hunting Card (*Metsästyskortti*).

---

## 🏛️ The Institutional Hierarchy

Finnish hunting administration operates under the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (*Maa- ja metsätalousministeriö*, MMM). The key bodies you will encounter are:

1. **Suomen riistakeskus (The Finnish Wildlife Agency)**:
   - The central independent operational authority.
   - Promotes sustainable game management, executes wildlife policy, grants special hunting derogations/permits, and oversees game research and monitoring.
   - Maintains the national digital hunting portal: **[Oma riista](https://oma.riista.fi)**.

2. **Riistanhoitoyhdistys (RHY - Game Management Association)**:
   - There are approximately 280 local RHYs across Finland.
   - Each RHY is the grassroots regulatory and educational body for its municipality or district.
   - **What they do for you**: They organize the preparatory courses, administer the Hunter's Exams, conduct mandatory shooting tests (*ampumakoe*), and train game stewards and youth.
   - When you pay your annual game management fee, you are registered as a member of your local RHY based on your home address.

3. **Suomen Metsästäjäliitto (The Finnish Hunters' Association)**:
   - The non-governmental voluntary advocacy organisation for hunters and hunting clubs (~150,000 members).
   - Publishes the national hunting magazine *Jahti*, runs shooting training (*Metsästysammunnan ABC*), organizes youth camps, and defends hunter rights.

## 📝 The Hunter's Examination (*Metsästäjätutkinto*)

Under Chapter 3, Section 17 of the Finnish Hunting Act (*Metsästyslaki*), anyone who wishes to hunt in Finland must demonstrate adequate knowledge of hunting legislation, game ecology, weapon safety, and species identification by passing the **Hunter's Examination**.

### 1. Structure of the Exam
The exam is a written multiple-choice test consisting of **60 questions**:
- **15 Species Identification Questions**: Identifying photos or illustrations of Finnish mammals and birds (including protected species, game species, non-game lookalikes, and invasive alien species).
- **45 Theory Questions**:
  - Hunting legislation and regulations (hunting seasons, protected zones, distances from buildings, transport of weapons).
  - Game ecology and wildlife population management.
  - Safe firearm and bow handling, shooting techniques, and accident prevention.
  - Hunting methods and dog breeds used in Finnish hunting.
  - Game care, field dressing, hygiene, and ethics.

### 2. Passing Criteria
- The maximum number of errors permitted is **8 out of 60**.
- **Crucial Rule**: You are allowed a maximum of **4 errors in the Species Identification section**, and a maximum of **4 errors in the Theory section**. If you make 5 errors in species identification (even if you get 100% in theory), you fail the exam.
- Time limit: Typically 60 to 90 minutes (plenty of time).

### 3. Language Availability: Can You Take It in English?
- **YES.** The exam can officially be taken in **English**, **Finnish**, **Swedish**, or **Northern Sámi**.
- When arriving at the examination session, inform the RHY exam invigilator in advance that you need the English test sheet.
- *Note on Study Material*: The primary official study manual, ***Metsästäjän opas*** (Hunter's Guide), is published in Finnish and Swedish by Suomen riistakeskus. English-speaking candidates typically prepare using:
  1. The Finnish *Metsästäjän opas* combined with Google Lens / visual translation tools.
  2. Online interactive courses on **[Riistainfo.fi](https://riistainfo.fi)** and **[Riistapolku.fi](https://riistapolku.fi)**.
  3. Attending an RHY preparatory weekend course (many instructors gladly explain concepts in English).
  4. The practice quizzes included in this project’s interactive web app.

### 4. Preparatory Courses (*Valmentava koulutus*)
- Local RHYs regularly organize 12-to-16-hour preparatory training courses before exam sessions (often over 2 to 4 evenings or a single intensive weekend).
- **Cost**: Usually between 30 € and 60 €.
- **Is it mandatory?** No, self-study is legally permitted. However, for a complete beginner, **attending a course is strongly recommended**. It gives you direct contact with experienced local hunters and clarifies tricky species identification (such as distinguishing a female mallard from a protected gadwall or pintail).

### 5. Exam Logistics & Costs
- **Finding an Exam**: Search the event calendar at **[riista.fi/metsastys/tapahtumahaku](https://riista.fi/metsastys/tapahtumahaku/)** under *Metsästäjätutkinto*.
- **Exam Fee**: **20 €** per attempt. (If you fail, you can retake the test at the next scheduled session by paying another 20 €).
- **What to Bring**:
  - Official photo ID (passport or Finnish identity card; note: driving licenses are sometimes accepted by RHY for this test, but bring a passport to be safe).
  - Cash or card for the 20 € fee.
  - Pen or pencil.

## 🪪 The Hunting Card (*Metsästyskortti*)

Once you pass the exam, the invigilator gives you a signed certificate and submits your results to the national hunter register (*Metsästäjärekisteri*).

### 1. Paying the Game Management Fee (*Riistanhoitomaksu*)
- Within a few days of passing, your record is active in **Oma riista**.
- You must pay the annual **Game Management Fee** (currently **43 €** for adults, **20 €** for youth under 18).
- The Finnish hunting year runs from **August 1st to July 31st**.

### 2. What the Hunting Card Gives You
1. **Legal Proof of Right to Hunt**: You must carry it (or show it via the Oma riista smartphone app) whenever hunting.
2. **Statutory Hunter's Insurance (*Metsästäjävakuutus*)**:
   - Every hunter with a valid card is automatically covered by third-party liability insurance.
   - Covers bodily injuries up to statutory limits and property damage caused during hunting or shooting tests.
   - Includes voluntary accident insurance for the hunter.
3. **Subscription to *Metsästäjä* Magazine**:
   - The official journal published 6 times a year (delivered in Finnish or Swedish), keeping you informed on quota changes, regulations, and wildlife surveys.

## 📱 The Digital Hub: *Oma riista*

Download the **Oma riista** mobile app (available on iOS and Android) and register at [oma.riista.fi](https://oma.riista.fi) using Finnish bank e-identification (*Suomi.fi tunnistus*).

**Features you will use:**
- **Electronic Hunting Card**: Displays your active status, hunter number (*metsästäjänumero*), and RHY affiliation.
- **Shooting Test Records**: Shows your valid rifle (*hirvikoe*) or bow (*jousikoe*) test credentials and expiry dates.
- **Harvest Log (*Saalispäiväkirja*)**: Instant digital logging of harvested animals (mandatory for quota species like roe deer, white-tailed deer, and waterfowl).
- **Territory Maps**: Downloadable boundaries of state hunting zones (*Metsähallitus*) and club leases.

## 🌍 Visiting / Foreign Hunters

If you already possess a valid hunting license or permit from your home country (e.g. Germany, UK, USA, France, Sweden), you can obtain a temporary Finnish Hunting Card without sitting the Finnish examination:
1. Submit a certified copy of your home country hunting license to the coordinator of the local Game Management Association where you plan to hunt.
2. The RHY will issue a payment order for the 43 € game management fee.
3. Once paid, a temporary Finnish hunting card is valid for one hunting season.
4. *Important*: If hunting moose, deer, or bear, you must still provide proof of passing an equivalent home-country shooting test on large game, or pass the Finnish shooting test (*ampumakoe*) on a local range.
