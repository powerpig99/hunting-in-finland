# 02. Firearms, Licensing & Supervised Shooting (*Ampuma-aselaki*)

In Finland, firearms ownership is strictly controlled by the National Police Board and local police departments (*Poliisi*). Firearms are never granted for generic self-defense; every permit requires a recognized, legitimate purpose, of which **hunting is the most common and respected**.

This guide demystifies the licensing process, the legal storage requirements, and the crucial provisions that allow beginners to practice and hunt **before** owning their own firearm.

---

## 🏛️ The Legal Basis & Who Grants Permits

Firearms in Finland are regulated under the Firearms Act (*Ampuma-aselaki 1/1998*). All permits are processed and issued by the **Finnish Police (*Poliisi*)**, not the Wildlife Agency.

### Core Eligibility Criteria:
1. **Age**: You must be at least **18 years old** to apply for an independent firearm permit. (Youth aged 15–17 may obtain a *parallel permit* with guardian consent).
2. **Suitability & Background Check**: The police thoroughly review criminal records, traffic offenses, healthcare records, substance abuse history, and military service discharge records (for Finnish citizens).
3. **Legitimate Need (Hunting Purpose)**: You must present a valid **Finnish Hunting Card (*Metsästyskortti*)** with proof of the paid game management fee.
4. **Weapon Suitability Principle (*Aseen soveltuvuus*)**: Under Finnish law, you cannot simply ask for "a gun." You must specify the weapon type, action mechanism, and caliber, and justify why it is appropriate for the species you intend to hunt.

## 🔍 Step-by-Step: Obtaining an Acquisition Permit (*Hankkimislupa*)

An acquisition permit gives you the legal right to purchase a specific firearm from a licensed gun dealer or private individual.

```
Step 1: Pass Hunter Exam & Pay Game Management Fee (Get Hunting Card)
   ↓
Step 2: Submit Electronic Application via Poliisi.fi
   ↓
Step 3: In-Person Police Interview & Document Verification
   ↓
Step 4: Police Issue Acquisition Permit (Hankkimislupa, valid for 1 year)
   ↓
Step 5: Purchase the Firearm from Gun Shop or Private Seller
   ↓
Step 6: Physical Inspection of the Firearm at the Police Station (within 30 days)
   ↓
Step 7: Police Issue Plastic Possession Permit (Hallussapitolupa)
```

### 1. Application Submission (Online or In-Person)
- You can apply via the police electronic portal (**[poliisi.fi](https://poliisi.fi)**) using your Finnish bank authentication (*Suomi.fi*).
- **Application Fee**: Approximately **106 € – 115 €** per firearm.
- **Documents Required**:
  - Valid Passport and Finnish residence permit card (pysyvä oleskelulupa / PR).
  - Valid Finnish Hunting Card (*metsästyskortti*) with proof of paid game management fee.
  - Written statement of hunting purpose (*perustelulomake*): Explain where you plan to hunt (e.g. Metsähallitus state lands in Kainuu/Lapland via Eräluvat, or a local hunting club in Uusimaa) and which species (e.g. waterfowl, forest birds, white-tailed deer).
  - **Military Service Requirement Clarification**: Finnish male citizens are asked for a military passport (*sotilaspassi*). **As a foreign permanent resident, you are legally exempt from Finnish conscription**, so this requirement is simply waived by the police. The police conduct their standard domestic background check through Finnish criminal, healthcare, and police registries.

### 2. The Police Interview
- First-time applicants are almost always invited for an in-person interview at the local police department (e.g. Pasila in Helsinki, or your local municipal station).
- The officer will ask about your living situation, where the gun will be stored (confirm you have an approved locked cabinet or EN 14450 safe), your hunting plans, and your understanding of firearm safety.
- You may be asked to complete a short electronic aptitude questionnaire on a police computer terminal (testing emotional stability, safety awareness, and suitability). The interview can be conducted in English or Finnish.

### 3. Purchasing and Physical Firearm Inspection (*Aseen esittäminen*)
- Once granted, the paper *hankkimislupa* is mailed to you (valid for 1 year).
- Take it to a gun retailer (e.g. Varuste.net, Ruoto, Sissos, Asejahti) to purchase your firearm.
- Within **30 days** of purchase, you must book an appointment at the police station to present the unloaded firearm in its locked case so the police can verify serial numbers, caliber, and barrel length.
- The police then issue your permanent **Firearms Card (*Asekortti / Hallussapitolupa*)**.

## 🎯 Which Firearm Should a Beginner Choose?

Finnish police will not grant a license for a large rifle if you only state you want to hunt mallards, nor a shotgun if you state you want to hunt moose. Here are the two standard beginner configurations:

### Option A: The All-Round 12-Gauge Shotgun (*Haulikko 12/76*)
- **Purpose**: Waterfowl (duck, goose), forest grouse (black grouse, capercaillie), hares, pheasant, and wood pigeon.
- **Action**: Over-and-Under (*päällekkäispiippuinen*) or Semi-Automatic (*puoliautomaatti*). Over-and-under double-barrel shotguns are universally favored by beginners for safety and simplicity.
- **Police Justification**: *"Pienriistan metsästys (vesilinnut, metsäkanalinnut, jänikset) Metsähallituksen valtionmailla ja vierasluvilla."*

### Option B: The Versatile Bolt-Action Rifle (*Kivääri .308 Winchester*)
- **Purpose**: Roe deer, white-tailed deer, moose, wild boar, and long-range seal/beaver.
- **Caliber**: **.308 Winchester** is by far the most popular hunting caliber in Finland. Ammunition is widely available, affordable, and fully meets legal energy requirements for both deer and moose.
- **Police Justification**: *"Hirvieläinten (valkohäntäpeura, hirvi) metsästys seurajahdeissa ja kytiksellä."*

> [!WARNING]
> **Handguns (Pistols & Revolvers)**: In Finland, handguns are subject to severe restrictions. For hunting, they are only granted under narrow conditions for dispatching trapped small predators or cave hunting (*luolakoirametsästys*), requiring proof of active trapping activity certified by an RHY. First-time hunters cannot obtain handguns.

## 🔒 Safe Storage Laws (*Aseen säilytys*)

Finnish law imposes strict criminal liability on the storage of firearms and ammunition:

1. **Location**: Firearms must be stored in the owner's permanent domestic residence. You cannot leave a gun in a summer cottage (*mökki*) when you are not residing there.
2. **Storage for 1–4 Long Guns**:
   - Must be stored in a locked cabinet or locked location, or a vital component (such as the rifle bolt or shotgun forend) must be stored in a separate locked place from the rest of the gun.
   - Ammunition must be kept in a separate locked compartment from the firearms.
3. **The 5-Gun Rule & Certified Safe (EN 14450)**:
   - If you own **5 or more firearms**, or even **one handgun / particularly dangerous firearm**, Finnish law makes a certified gun safe **mandatory**.
   - The safe must meet European standard **EN 14450 (Level S1 or S2)** or **EN 1143-1**.
   - If the safe weighs less than **150 kg empty**, it must be securely bolted to the wall studs or concrete floor.

## 💡 How Beginners Can Hunt & Shoot WITHOUT Owning a Gun

You do not need to invest thousands of euros or wait months for police permits to start shooting or hunting! Finnish law provides two key mechanisms:

### 1. Supervised Shooting & Guided Hunting (*Valvottu ammunta / Valvottu käyttö*)
Under Chapter 6, Section 88 of the Firearms Act:
- Any person may shoot with a firearm **without a permit** if they are under the **immediate personal supervision** of a licensed permit holder.
- **Legal Requirements for the Supervisor**:
  - Must be at least 18 years old.
  - Must hold a valid possession permit (*hallussapitolupa*) for that specific gun.
  - Must maintain continuous visual and physical contact, standing within **arm's reach** (*käsivarren mitan päässä*) and capable of immediately intervening.
- **Practical Application**:
  - **Clay & Rifle Ranges**: You can visit shooting clubs (e.g. Sipoo, Lahnus, Malmi simulator, or regional sporting clays clubs) and rent club shotguns to practice shooting under a certified range officer’s supervision.
  - **Guided Hunts**: An outfitter or experienced mentor can walk beside you in the field. You hold the gun under their direct watchful eye to harvest waterfowl or small game!

### 2. Parallel Permit (*Rinnakkaislupa*)
- If a close friend, spouse, parent, or hunting partner already owns a hunting shotgun or rifle, you do not need to buy a duplicate weapon.
- With their written permission, you apply to the police for a **parallel permit** (*rinnakkaislupa*).
- **Benefits**: Application fee is lower (~40 €–60 €), no new gun purchase is necessary, and once granted, you have independent possession rights to that firearm for hunting trips.

### 3. Borrowing Firearms (*Aseen lainaaminen*)
- *Important Distinction*: Under Finnish law, you may only borrow a firearm for independent use if you **already hold a valid possession permit for that category of weapon**.
- A person with no firearm permits cannot borrow a gun to go into the woods alone.
