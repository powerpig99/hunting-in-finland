# 05. Finnish Game Species, Seasons & Quotas

Finland offers an extraordinary variety of game species adapted to the boreal taiga, agricultural landscapes, coastal archipelagos, and subarctic fells.

Understanding the biology, seasonal cycles, permitted harvest methods, and ballistic requirements for each species is essential for ethical, legal hunting.

---

## 📅 The Finnish Hunting Calendar Overview

The Finnish hunting year begins on **August 1st**. The autumn season kicks off with two massive national milestones:
- **August 10 at 00:00**: Wood pigeon season opens (field decoy hunting).
- **August 20 at 12:00 NOON**: Waterfowl season opens (celebrated nationwide on lakes and coastal marshes).
- **September 1**: Roe deer, hares, and forest birds open.
- **September / October**: Moose and white-tailed deer hunts commence.
- **Mid-January to March**: Late-winter hare tracking on skis, predator hunting, and snow scouting.

## 🦃 1. Small Game Birds

### Forest Grouse (*Metsäkanalinnut*)
The crown jewels of the Finnish boreal forest. Their numbers fluctuate on a 6-to-7-year cycle depending on spring weather and blueberry crops.
- **Capercaillie (*Metso*, *Tetrao urogallus*)**: The giant of the forest (males weigh 4–5 kg). Hunted through stalking in mature pine ridges or with barking spitz dogs.
- **Black Grouse (*Teeri*, *Lyrurus tetrix*)**: Distinctive lyre-shaped tail feathers. Hunted on peat bogs and forest edges using decoys mounted on tall birch poles.
- **Hazel Grouse (*Pyy*, *Tetrastes bonasia*)**: Small, monogamous grouse inhabiting spruce valleys with alder trees. Hunted by imitating its territorial whistle using a small metal pipe (*pyypilli*).
- **Willow Grouse (*Riekko*, *Lagopus lagopus*)**: Found primarily in the fells and peatlands of Lapland, turning completely snow-white in winter.
- **Typical Season**: September 10 to November/December (exact dates and regional closures set each August by the Ministry based on census triangles).

### Waterfowl (*Vesilinnut*)
- **Key Species**: Mallard (*Sinisorsa*), Eurasian Teal (*Tavi*), Eurasian Wigeon (*Haapana*), Common Goldeneye (*Telkkä*), and Canada Goose (*Kanadanhanhi*).
- **Protected Species Warning**: You must learn to distinguish protected ducks (e.g. Pintail hen, Garganey, Velvet Scoter) before pulling the trigger.
- **Season**: August 20 at 12:00 noon until December 31.
- **Mandatory Lead-Shot Ban**: Under EU REACH legislation, **lead shot is strictly prohibited in or within 100 meters of any wetland or body of water**. Hunters must use steel, bismuth, copper, or tungsten-polymer ammunition.

### Wood Pigeon (*Sepelkyyhky*)
- High-volume, fast-paced shooting over freshly harvested grain or pea fields using decoy spreads and camouflage hides.
- Season opens August 10. Excellent culinary meat (dark, lean, rich breast fillets).

## 🐇 2. Small Mammals

### Mountain Hare (*Metsäjänis*) vs. European Brown Hare (*Rusakko*)
- **Mountain Hare (*Metsäjänis*)**: Native to Finnish forests and bogs. Turns pure white in winter (except black ear tips). Hunted traditionally with scent hounds (Finnish Hound / *suomenajokoira*).
- **European Brown Hare (*Rusakko*)**: Colonized Southern and Central Finland from the south. Larger (up to 6 kg), remains grayish-brown all winter, and populates farm fields and suburban gardens.
- **Season**: September 1 to the end of February (brown hare often extended into March).

### Eurasian & Canadian Beaver (*Euroopanmajava & Kanadanmajava*)
- **Eurasian Beaver**: Native, protected in most areas; requires special quota permits (Satakunta region).
- **Canadian Beaver**: Introduced alien species, common in Eastern Finland. Hunted in spring (March to April) along thawed creeks with rifle or bow.

## 🦌 3. Big Game (*Hirvieläimet*)

### The Moose (*Hirvi*, *Alces alces*)
- **The National Big Game Animal**: Adult bulls weigh between 400 and 600 kg.
- **Hunting Method**: Communal club hunts (*seurajahti*) utilizing loose-running hunting dogs (Jämtland Hound, Norwegian Elkhound) that track moose and bark to hold them stationary until the hunter stalks in, or driving lines of standers (*passimiehet*).
- **Permit Requirement**: Quota permit (*hirvilupa*) issued by the Wildlife Agency to a registered hunting club with at least 1,000 hectares of contiguous land.
- **Season**: In northern Lapland opens in September; nationwide opens early October and continues through December/January.

### White-Tailed Deer (*Valkohäntäpeura*, *Odocoileus virginianus*)
- **Origin**: 5 animals were gifted to Finland by Finnish-American immigrants from Minnesota in 1934. Today, the population exceeds 100,000 animals, concentrated in Southwest Finland, Uusimaa, and Pirkanmaa.
- **Hunting Methods**:
  - **High-Seat Ambush (*Kyttäysjahti*)**: Sitting in heated, elevated blinds over grain/apple feeding stations at dawn or dusk.
  - **Driven Hunts with Small Dogs**: Short-legged hounds (Dachshunds, Drevers, Bassets) drive deer slowly past hunters on stand.
  - **Bow Hunting**: Stalking or ground blinds along field edges.
- **Season**: September 1 to February 15.

### Roe Deer (*Metsäkauris*, *Capreolus capreolus*)
- Europe’s smallest deer (15–30 kg). Delicacy venison.
- **No License Quota Required**: Unlike moose and white-tailed deer, roe deer do **not** require a quota license from the Wildlife Agency! You only need landowner/club permission.
- **Spring Buck Season**: May 16 to June 15 (bucks only, calling with buttolo calls).
- **General Season**: September 1 to February 15.
- **Reporting**: Every harvested roe deer must be logged in Oma riista within 7 days.
- **Weapons**: Legal with rifle, shotgun with buckshot, or hunting bow (with passed bow test).

## 🦝 4. Invasive Alien Carnivores & Wildlife Conservation

Under Finnish law, certain invasive alien species threaten native biodiversity, particularly ground-nesting waterbirds in coastal archipelagos:
- **Raccoon Dog (*Supikoira*)**: Native to East Asia, spread across the USSR into Finland. Prolific egg and chick predator, and a primary vector for rabies and echinococcus.
- **American Mink (*Minkki*)**: Escaped from fur farms, decimated native shorebirds and fish.
- **Hunting Status**: Classified as invasive alien species (*haitallinen vieraslaji*). Can be trapped or hunted year-round using humane kill traps, earthdogs (*luolakoirat*), or night-vision optics on feeding sites. Hunting these pests is considered valuable conservation work.

## 🎯 5. The Rifle Shooting Test (*Ampumakoe*)

To hunt moose, deer, wild boar, or bear with a rifle, you must pass the mandatory **Shooting Test (*Ampumakoe*)**, administered by an RHY.

### Test Format:
- **Target**: Life-sized stationary moose target (*hirvikuvio*).
- **Distance**: **75 meters**.
- **Execution**:
  - **4 shots** fired within **90 seconds** (timer starts upon first shot).
  - Positions allowed: Standing, kneeling, or sitting. (Prone/lying down is **not** permitted).
  - Shooting supports allowed: Shooting sticks (*ampumakeppi*), bipods, slings, or resting against range wooden posts. (Clamping/locking the gun mechanically to a bench is prohibited).
- **Passing Criterion**: All 4 shots must strike within the circular **23 cm diameter hit ring**.
- **Validity**: **3 years** from the date of passing. Automatically appears in your Oma riista app.
- **Cost**: **20 €** per attempt.

### Ballistic Minimums for Big Game Rifles (Hunting Decree § 16a):
To hunt moose, bear, or large deer, your rifle cartridge must meet legal kinetic energy thresholds measured at 100 meters ($E_{100}$):
- **Moose, Bear, Wild Boar**:
  - Bullet weight $\ge 6.0\text{ g}$ and $E_{100} \ge 2,000\text{ Joules}$; OR
  - Bullet weight $\ge 8.0\text{ g}$ and $E_{100} \ge 1,700\text{ Joules}$; OR
  - **Lead-Free Bullets (Copper/Brass)**: Bullet weight $\ge 5.1\text{ g}$ and $E_{100} \ge 1,700\text{ Joules}$.
- *.308 Winchester, 6.5x55 SE, .30-06 Springfield, and 9.3x62 Mauser* all easily satisfy these requirements.
